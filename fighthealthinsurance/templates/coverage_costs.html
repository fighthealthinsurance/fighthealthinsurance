Price changes are list prices, before premium help. The three price rows measure different things, so compare each across years, not with each other. Out-of-pocket limits don't include premiums.

  • It differs by state. Benchmark prices rise 11% in states that use HealthCare.gov and 16% in states that run their own platforms. (Final, KFF, October 30, 2026)
  • Check more than the premium. CMS says more than 75% of people buying through HealthCare.gov can get premiums at or below $50 in 2027. A low premium can come with a high deductible. (Official, CMS, October 30, 2026)
  • Most people get premium help. About 87% of people who picked a plan for 2026 got premium tax credits. (Official, CMS, March 2026)
  • Help rises with prices. It's based on your income and your area's benchmark plan, the second-lowest-cost silver plan. When list prices rise, your help usually does too.
  • No federal premium tax credit above 400% of the poverty level. Some states have their own help; your state's marketplace can tell you. (Official, IRS, July 2026)
  • For many people, the big jump came in 2026. The extra premium help ended after 2025. The average premium after help rose from $113 a month for 2025 to $178 for 2026. So 2026 to 2027 understates what long-time enrollees felt. (Official, CMS, May 2025 and March 2026)

Job-based plans

Your employer decides how much of the premium you pay.

How we got these numbers: Job-based plans

  • Average yearly premium, 2026: $9,804 for one person and $28,303 for a family. Each rose 5% from 2025. (Survey, KFF, October 22, 2026)
  • What workers paid, 2026: $1,672 for one person and $7,471 for a family. The family share rose 9% from 2025. (Survey, KFF, October 22, 2026)
  • Expected for 2027: employers expect their health benefit cost per employee to rise 8.2% on average, even after changes such as higher deductibles. (Projected, Marsh, September 2, 2026)

Averages for what workers pay in 2027 aren't out yet. Your enrollment materials show your own.

What you can do

What to do now

  • See marketplace prices. HealthCare.gov shows plans and prices for your area; 2027 open enrollment runs through January 15.
  • Use your state's marketplace if it has one. Find yours on HealthCare.gov's list.
  • Compare Medicare plans by December 7. Medicare Plan Finder checks plans against your drugs and pharmacies.
  • Compare federal plans in Open Season. Use OPM's plan comparison tool and premium charts.
  • On a job-based plan, ask HR. Each plan's Summary of Benefits and Coverage shows what you'd pay for care.

  • Get free help. Find an independent assister, or your state's free Medicare counseling on our state help pages.

Sources and method

Each number is as its source published it, except the 87%, which is our math from CMS's counts. Each date is when the source published it, or when a data file's page was last updated.

Marketplace plans

  1. Benchmark price change: KFF, Matt McGough and others, "9 Things to Watch for the 2027 ACA Open Enrollment Period", October 30, 2026. The average change in finalized benchmark silver premiums, weighted across all 50 states and DC, from HealthCare.gov, state rate review sites, state plan finders and state data.
  2. Premiums at or below $50: CMS, Plan Year 2027 Marketplace Plans and Prices Fact Sheet, October 30, 2026. HealthCare.gov only. It doesn't give an average price change, or say whether the $50 is after premium help.
  3. Median price change: KFF, a nonprofit health policy research, polling and news organization. Matt McGough and others, "How Much and Why ACA Marketplace Premiums Are Going Up in 2027", July 8, 2026, updated August 3, 2026. The median of 276 insurers' proposed changes in all 50 states and DC. For 2026, KFF gives 18% proposed and 20% final. We cite KFF's figures and don't copy its charts.
  4. Weighted price change: Families USA, a national consumer health advocacy organization, "ACA Marketplace Premium Rate Tracker", updated October 26, 2026. Requested changes in all 50 states and DC, weighted by enrollment, and 2026's average final change. It updates as states finalize rates.
  5. Premium help: the IRS's yearly tables of the share of income people pay for the benchmark plan, which stop at 400% of the poverty level: Rev. Proc. 2025-25 for 2026 and Rev. Proc. 2026-26 for 2027.
  6. Out-of-pocket limits: CMS, 2027 payment parameters guidance, January 29, 2026, with the 2026 and 2027 limits.
  7. Premium help and what people paid: CMS's Marketplace Open Enrollment Period public use files for 2025 (page updated May 12, 2025) and 2026 (March 27, 2026). The state-level file's national row, HealthCare.gov and state-run marketplaces together. The average is the monthly premium per person after premium tax credits, for everyone with a plan picked. It leaves out extra help some states add, and it moves with the plans people pick. The 87% is the 20,035,756 people who got a premium tax credit out of the 23,130,860 who picked a plan.

Job-based plans

  1. Premiums and workers' share: KFF, 2026 Employer Health Benefits Survey, October 22, 2026. A yearly survey of employers; national averages, the employer's share included.
  2. 2027 costs: Marsh, preliminary results of its 2026 National Survey of Employer-Sponsored Health Plans, from more than 1,800 employers, announced September 2, 2026 on the site of Mercer, which is becoming Marsh. Total health benefit cost per employee, after the changes employers plan.

Medicare

  1. Part B for 2026: CMS, 2026 Medicare Parts A & B Premiums and Deductibles, November 14, 2025.
  2. Part B for 2027: the 2026 Medicare Trustees Report, June 2026, table V.E2. It estimates the standard premium and deductible; CMS sets the official amounts later.
  3. Medicare Advantage and drug plan premiums: CMS, "Medicare Advantage and Medicare Prescription Drug Programs Expected to Remain Stable in 2027", September 28, 2026. Averages weighted by enrollment. The Medicare Advantage average includes plans with drug coverage and Special Needs Plans. The 2027 averages are CMS's projections.
  4. Part D limits: CMS, 2027 Rate Announcement, April 6, 2026, which sets 2027's deductible and out-of-pocket threshold and lists 2026's.
