Some 2027 prices are set. Others are still what insurers asked for, or an estimate, and a few come out in November. Each number below says which, who published it and when. What you pay depends on your plan, where you live, your age and, on the marketplace, your income, so check your own price before you choose.

What the labels mean:

  • Official: set or counted by a government agency.
  • Final: the rates insurers charge after review.
  • Proposed: what insurers asked for, which can change before it's final.
  • Projected: an estimate made before the real number is out.
  • Survey: an average from a survey of employers.

For what else changes in 2027, and a fall checklist, see Preparing for 2027.

  • Most people with a marketplace plan get premium help. About 87% of people who picked a plan for 2026 got premium tax credits. (Official, CMS, March 2026)
  • Premium help rises with prices. It's set by your income and the price of the benchmark plan in your area, the second-lowest-cost silver plan. When that price goes up, your help usually does too, so a list-price increase usually isn't what you pay.
  • There's no premium help above 400% of the poverty level. With an income above that in 2027, you pay the full list price. (Official, IRS, July 2026)
  • For many people, the big jump came from 2025 to 2026, when the extra premium help ended. For plans picked in open enrollment, the average premium after help went from $113 a month for 2025 to $178 for 2026, so the change from 2026 to 2027 understates what long-time enrollees have felt. (Official, CMS, May 2025 and March 2026)

Job-based plans

Coverage through your job or a family member's job. Your employer decides how much of the premium you pay.

  • Average yearly premium in 2026: $9,804 for one person and $28,303 for a family, each 5% more than in 2025. (Survey, KFF, October 22, 2026)
  • Workers' share of that in 2026: $1,672 a year for one person and $7,471 for a family, with the family share 9% more than in 2025. (Survey, KFF, October 22, 2026)
  • For 2027: employers expect their health benefit cost per employee to rise 8.2% on average, even after changes they plan, such as higher deductibles. (Projected, Marsh, September 2, 2026)

Averages for what workers pay in 2027 aren't out yet. Your employer's open enrollment materials show your own.

Where to see your own price

  • HealthCare.gov: see plans and prices for your area, with savings estimated from your income, before you log in.

  • A state marketplace: if your state runs its own, find its site from HealthCare.gov's list.

  • Medicare: Medicare Plan Finder compares Medicare Advantage and drug plans, with your own drugs and pharmacies.

  • Federal employees and retirees: OPM's plan comparison tool and premium charts, for FEHB and PSHB plans.

  • A job-based plan: your employer's open enrollment materials show what comes out of your paycheck. For what you'd pay when you get care, ask HR for each plan's Summary of Benefits and Coverage, a short summary every plan has to give you.

  • Free help choosing: find an independent assister for marketplace plans, or your state's free Medicare counseling program on our state help pages.

Sources and method

Numbers and links checked October 28, 2026.

Each number is as its source published it, except the 87%, which is our math from CMS's counts. Each date is when the source published it, or for a data file, when its page was last updated.

Marketplace plans

  1. Price change for the typical insurer: KFF, a nonprofit health policy research, polling and news organization. Matt McGough and others, "How Much and Why ACA Marketplace Premiums Are Going Up in 2027", first published July 8, 2026 and updated August 3, 2026. The 2027 figure is the median of the changes 276 insurers proposed in all 50 states and DC. For 2026, KFF gives a median of 18% proposed and 20% final. We cite KFF's figures; we don't copy its charts.
  2. Average price change: Families USA, a national consumer health advocacy organization, "ACA Marketplace Premium Rate Tracker", last updated October 26, 2026. The change insurers asked for across all 50 states and DC, weighted by enrollment, and the average final change for 2026. The tracker changes as states finalize rates.
  3. Premium help: the IRS's tables of how much of their income people getting premium help pay for the benchmark plan, which stop at 400% of the poverty level: Rev. Proc. 2025-25 for 2026 and Rev. Proc. 2026-26 for 2027.
  4. Out-of-pocket limits: CMS, 2027 payment parameters guidance, January 29, 2026, which gives the 2026 and 2027 limits.
  5. Who got premium help, and what people paid: CMS's Marketplace Open Enrollment Period public use files for 2025 (page updated May 12, 2025) and 2026 (March 27, 2026). From the state-level file's national row, HealthCare.gov and state-run marketplaces together: the average monthly premium per person after premium tax credits, for everyone with a plan picked, including people who got no credit. Extra help that some state-run marketplaces add isn't counted, and the averages move with the plans people pick as well as with prices. The 87% is the 20,035,756 people who got a premium tax credit out of the 23,130,860 who picked a plan.

Job-based plans

  1. Premiums and workers' share: KFF, 2026 Employer Health Benefits Survey, October 22, 2026, a yearly survey of employers. National averages, including the employer's share.
  2. 2027 costs: Marsh, the preliminary results of its 2026 National Survey of Employer-Sponsored Health Plans, from more than 1,800 employers, announced September 2, 2026 on the site of Mercer, which is becoming Marsh. Total health benefit cost per employee, after the changes employers plan.

Federal employees and retirees

  1. Premium increases and Open Season: OPM, Federal Benefits Open Season Highlights, 2027 Plan Year, September 2026, which gives the average changes across all plans for 2026 and 2027.

Medicare

  1. Part B for 2026: CMS, 2026 Medicare Parts A & B Premiums and Deductibles, November 14, 2025.
  2. Part B for 2027: the 2026 Medicare Trustees Report, June 2026, which estimates the 2027 standard premium and deductible (table V.E2). CMS announces the official amounts later.
  3. Medicare Advantage and drug plan premiums: CMS, "Medicare Advantage and Medicare Prescription Drug Programs Expected to Remain Stable in 2027", September 28, 2026. Averages weighted by enrollment; the Medicare Advantage average includes plans with drug coverage and Special Needs Plans, and the 2027 averages are CMS's projections.
  4. Part D limits: CMS, 2027 Rate Announcement, April 6, 2026, which sets the 2027 deductible and out-of-pocket threshold and lists 2026's.

Still to come

  2. Timing: we expect CMS's files and the Part B announcement around when last year's came out. CMS released the 2026 Part B amounts on November 14, 2025.
