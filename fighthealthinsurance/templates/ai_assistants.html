Other assistants and your own models

Anything that speaks MCP works. Look for a setting called Connectors, Integrations, Tools, Plugins, Extensions or MCP servers, with an option to add a custom or remote server. If you can't find it, search your app's help pages for "MCP".

Wherever it asks, enter:

  • Address: https://www.fighthealthinsurance.com/mcp
  • Type or transport: Streamable HTTP. Some apps call it HTTP, or just URL.
  • Sign-in or authentication: none.

Some apps can only use MCP servers that run on your own computer. Those can't connect to ours directly.

Two we have tried from the command line, and one from VS Code's own docs:

  • Claude Code: claude mcp add --transport http fight-health-insurance https://www.fighthealthinsurance.com/mcp
  • Codex CLI: codex mcp add fight-health-insurance --url https://www.fighthealthinsurance.com/mcp
  • VS Code (1.100 or later, with Copilot): code --add-mcp "{\"name\":\"fight-health-insurance\",\"type\":\"http\",\"url\":\"https://www.fighthealthinsurance.com/mcp\"}"

Apps that run open-weight models on your own machine take MCP servers too. Each app's guide shows where to paste the address: LM Studio, Open WebUI, Jan and LibreChat. Ollama on its own has no MCP support; Ollama users can connect through a front end such as Open WebUI. Two things to know: the model has to support tool calling, and small local models sometimes skip the tools or misread what they return, so check the links in the answer against this site.

If an answer doesn't link back to this site, try naming us: "Using Fight Health Insurance, what does 'not medically necessary' mean?"

Starting an appeal from your chat

The simplest: ask your assistant for the link to start an appeal here, then upload or paste your letter on our site yourself. Your assistant doesn't send us your letter.

{% if chat_path_on %}For the other two, share your denial letter with your assistant and ask it to help you appeal.{% else %}Or share your denial letter with your assistant and ask it to fill in our form for you.{% endif %} Anything you share in the chat, including your letter, also goes to the company that makes your assistant, under its own privacy policy. We ask your assistant to check with you before it sends us your letter. We also ask it to offer to swap your name, member ID and group number for placeholders and to leave out your address, phone number and date of birth. Say yes to that. If you agree, it sends us the letter, plus a few words on what was denied and the condition if it has them, and gives you a link. We ask it to tell you what it sent, too.

That link opens our page. The two hours start when your assistant makes the link, not when you open it.

Get letters back in your chat

We draft appeal letters, and your assistant can bring them back to the chat.

  1. Open the link and press Open my appeal form. Our page shows the letter your assistant sent.
  2. Check the letter. Under About you, fill in your name, email address, street address and ZIP code, then press Remove personal details. We try to keep your name and street address in your browser. Read the letter again and take out anything it missed, such as your member ID.
  3. Say who the appeal is for: you, or someone you're helping who asked you to and is fine with us keeping it as our privacy policy says.
  4. Tick the four boxes, which say: you've taken your personal details out of the letter, you've read our privacy policy, you agree to our terms, and the appeal is your own or for someone who asked you to. Then tick "I'm not a robot" if it shows.
  5. Under Optional choices, "Use outside AI services to get more appeal drafts" is ticked by default. While it's ticked, your letter is also shared with those services, under their own terms. You can untick it.
  6. Press Agree and go back to my chat. Then tell your assistant you're done.
  7. We read the letter and may have a few questions. Your assistant is asked to put them to you and send only what you say. You can skip any.
  8. Up to three draft letters come back to the chat, usually in a few minutes. Where a letter needs your name or member ID, it should have a placeholder. Your assistant is asked to fill those in from your chat, or ask you, and not to send them to us.
  9. Nothing is sent to your insurer. Read the letters, check the details, pick one, edit it and send it yourself.

Your assistant can collect the letters for a day after you agree. We also email you a link to finish on our site instead. It asks for your email address and works for 30 days.

Changed your mind on our page? Finish on this site instead opens our usual appeal form with your letter in it.
