How to get your full plan documents

You can ask your plan for its full documents, not just a summary. Here is how.

Plans through a private employer

Most job-based plans at private companies follow a federal law called ERISA. Plans from government employers and most churches don't.

  • You can ask in writing for the plan's key documents: the Summary Plan Description, the full plan document, the insurance policy if the plan is insured, the latest annual report (Form 5500), and any changes made since the Summary Plan Description.
  • A covered spouse or child can ask too, and some former employees can as well.
  • Send your request to the plan administrator. Their name and address are in your Summary Plan Description. It may be your employer rather than the insurance company on your card. No Summary Plan Description? Search for your plan's Form 5500 on the Department of Labor's free Form 5500 search. It names the administrator.
  • They have 30 days to mail the documents. If they don't, a judge can order them to pay you up to $110 a day. You would have to go to court to get it.
  • They can charge for paper copies, but no more than 25 cents a page, and no postage or handling fees. You can ask what it will cost first.
  • Small plans may not file an annual report, so there may be none to send.
  • If they don't answer, the Department of Labor's Employee Benefits Security Administration can help: askebsa.dol.gov or 1-866-444-3272.

Send your letter by certified mail with a return receipt, so you can show when they got it, and keep a copy.

Other kinds of plans

  • Job-based and individual plans: you can ask for a Summary of Benefits and Coverage at any time, and the plan or insurer must send it within 7 business days. It is only a summary. One from an insurer must list a web address for the full policy. For a job-based plan, that address is for the certificate of coverage, which is not the full group policy.
  • Marketplace plans on HealthCare.gov: the Summary of Benefits and Coverage is linked from each plan's page on HealthCare.gov when you preview plans. The full policy is at the web address in it. If your state runs its own marketplace, or you bought your plan straight from an insurer, ask the insurer for it.
  • Government and most church employer plans: ERISA doesn't cover these, so ask the benefits office for the plan's governing document. State and local government plans must still give you a Summary of Benefits and Coverage.
  • Federal employees: your plan's brochure on OPM's site is the official statement of benefits.
  • Medicare Advantage: your plan sends an Annual Notice of Change by September 30, and its Evidence of Coverage, or a notice of how to get it, by October 15. The plan posts it online and must mail you a paper copy if you ask.
  • Medicaid managed care: your plan must give you a member handbook. If it's online, the plan must mail you a free paper copy within 5 business days of asking.

After a denial

Ask for your claim file. Job-based plans covered by ERISA, and individual and Marketplace plans that aren't grandfathered, must give you free copies of the documents about your claim. That includes any internal rule or guideline they used to deny it.
