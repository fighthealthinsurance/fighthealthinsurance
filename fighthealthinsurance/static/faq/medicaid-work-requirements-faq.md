*Checked October 16, 2026, against the federal law and CMS's June 2026 rule. Details vary by state, and your state's notices have the final word.*

### Who has to meet the rule? {#who-has-to-meet-the-rule}
Adults 19 to 64 who got Medicaid through the ACA expansion (40 states and Washington, D.C.), and some adults in special programs in Georgia, Tennessee and Wisconsin. Most states start on January 1, 2027, and a few started earlier. [KFF's tracker](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/medicaid-work-requirements-tracker-overview) follows each state.

### How do I meet it? {#how-do-i-meet-it}
Any one of these counts for a month:
- 80 hours of work, approved job training, school, or volunteering with a nonprofit or public agency, in any mix.
- School at least half-time on its own: college, a trade program, high school or a GED program.
- At least $580 in household income that month (80 hours at the federal minimum wage). Unemployment pay counts as income. Seasonal workers can use their 6-month average.

### Who is exempt? {#who-is-exempt}
Federal law exempts these groups in every state:
- People who are pregnant or in their postpartum coverage.
- Parents, guardians and family caregivers of a child 13 or younger, or of someone with a disability.
- People with a disability, serious illness, disabling mental health condition or substance use disorder that makes the rule hard to meet. If your condition isn't on your state's list, you can ask.
- People in a drug or alcohol treatment program.
- American Indians and Alaska Natives, veterans with a total disability rating, and former foster youth under 26.
- People who meet SNAP or TANF work rules.
- People in jail or prison, and for 3 months after release.

People on Medicare aren't covered by the rule. Some states also excuse a month for a hospital stay, travel for serious medical care, or living in a county with a federal disaster or very high unemployment.

### How will my state check? {#how-will-my-state-check}
Your state has to check its own records first, such as payroll data, and ask you only when it can't confirm. It checks when you apply and at each renewal, which comes every 6 months starting in 2027. Keep pay stubs, school records and volunteer hours in case it asks.

### What if I get a notice? {#what-if-i-get-a-notice}
- If the notice says you haven't met the rule, you have 30 days to show you meet it or are exempt, counted from about 5 days after the date on the notice. Your coverage continues meanwhile.
- Before ending coverage, your state must check whether you qualify another way, then give you at least 10 days' notice and the right to a fair hearing.
- **To keep Medicaid while you appeal, ask for a hearing before the end date on your notice.** Your notice says how long you have to ask for a hearing at all, and some states allow less than 90 days. If you ask after the end date, coverage may stop while you wait.
- If you send what was missing within 90 days, your state must take another look without a new application. You can reapply any time you meet the rule.
- If you lose Medicaid this way, you also can't get financial help for a Marketplace plan.

### Where can I get help? {#where-can-i-get-help}
- Your state's Medicaid agency: find it on our [state help pages](/state-help/).
- Our [chat](/chat) can look up your state's Medicaid contacts, and its experimental eligibility check gives a rough estimate. Only your state can decide for real.
- Legal aid groups can help with a hearing.
